### Introduction

You've finished your Final Capstone, and you can now build, evaluate and deploy agents. The next job is getting someone to pay you for it. That starts with knowing which jobs exist. Job titles in AI are new and inconsistent: one company's "AI engineer" is another's "applied AI engineer," "agent engineer" or "software engineer, LLM." Reading past the titles to the actual work lets you aim your portfolio and your applications at roles you can win.

This lesson maps the main roles, what each one does day to day, and which parts of this curriculum prepared you for it.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- Where the "AI engineer" role came from and how it differs from ML engineering.
- AI engineer and agent engineer roles.
- Forward-deployed engineers and solutions engineers.
- ML engineers, research engineers and data scientists.
- Adjacent roles: AI product engineer, developer relations, evals and safety.
- Startups versus large companies.
- Choosing a target role.

### Key ideas

- **The AI engineer builds products on top of foundation models.** The role grew up around 2023 as the work shifted from training models to using them well: prompting and context engineering, retrieval, tool use, evals and shipping. This is the role this curriculum targets most directly.
- **Agent engineers are AI engineers who specialize in autonomous systems:** tool design, agent loops, MCP, memory, multi-agent orchestration, sandboxing and agent evaluation. The title is new, but the skills match your Agentic Systems path.
- **Forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) build with customers.** They embed with a client's team to ship AI solutions on the client's real data and systems, mixing engineering, consulting and communication. Demand for FDEs grew sharply as AI companies sold to enterprises. Strong writing, demos and the ability to scope a messy problem matter as much as code.
- **ML engineers and research engineers work closer to the model.** They train, fine-tune, evaluate and serve models, and they usually need more of the math and PyTorch depth from the optional ML Foundations path. Data scientists focus on analysis, experiments and classic models.
- **Adjacent roles are real entry points.** AI product engineers own a feature end to end, often including frontend. Developer relations roles reward people who write and teach well. Eval and AI safety roles reward the testing discipline you built in the Evals and Human-in-the-Loop & Safety sections.
- **Company size changes the job.** At a startup you'll be expected to do everything from database to demo. At a large company the scope is narrower and the bar for production reliability is higher.
- **Pick one target role for now.** A focused portfolio and resume beat a generic one. You can change course later; you can't aim at everything at once.
