### Introduction

A list of repos is not a portfolio. Reviewers rarely clone your code; they skim a page, watch part of a video, and decide whether to talk to you. A case study turns a project into a story they can follow in two minutes: the problem, what you built, how you know it works, and what you'd do next. A short demo video shows the thing working without asking anyone to set it up.

This lesson shows you how to write case studies and record demos for AI projects, where the evidence (evals, costs, failure cases) is what separates you from people who followed a tutorial.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- The structure of a strong case study.
- Choosing which projects to showcase.
- Showing evidence: eval results, costs, latency and failure analysis.
- Writing a README that works as a landing page.
- Planning and recording a short demo video.
- Making projects easy to try with live links.

### Key ideas

- **A case study follows a simple arc:** the problem and who has it, your approach and key decisions, the evidence it works, what went wrong and what you learned, and links to the repo, live demo and video. Keep it to one screen of reading, with details linked below.
- **Showcase depth, not count.** Three strong case studies beat ten thin repos. Your Final Capstone is case study number one. Pick two more that show different skills, for example an agent project and a production or evaluation project.
- **Evidence is the headline.** Put the eval score, the test set size, the cost per 1,000 calls and the latency near the top. Show at least one failure and how you handled it. In AI work, knowing where your system breaks is a senior skill.
- **Your README is the front door.** State what the project does in one sentence, show a screenshot or GIF, give one-command setup, and link the case study. Write it for a reviewer, not for yourself.
- **Demo videos should be three minutes or less.** Open with the problem in one sentence, show the happy path, show one hard case or failure being handled, flash the eval results, and stop. Script it, record your screen with your voice, and do a few takes. Nobody needs studio quality, but they do need clear audio.
- **Reduce friction to try it.** A live deployment (from Hello, Production or Ship It) is ideal. If running it costs you money, add rate limits or a recorded demo instead, and say so.

### Assignment

<div class="lesson-content__panel" markdown="1">

1. Read [Make a README](https://www.makeareadme.com/) for a practical checklist of what a good README contains.
   - Use it to audit your Capstone README, and fix the two weakest sections.
1. Reread the "eval" sections of [Hamel Husain's Your AI Product Needs Evals](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/) with a case-study lens: notice how he presents evidence that a system improved.
1. Install [OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com/), a free, open-source screen recorder that works on Windows, macOS and Linux.
   - Set up a scene with your screen and microphone, record a 30-second test, and check that the audio is clear.
1. Write a case study for your Final Capstone using the arc above, and record a demo video of three minutes or less. Ask one person who hasn't seen the project to watch it and tell you what it does. If they can't, revise.
