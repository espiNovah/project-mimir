### Introduction

Your resume has one job: get you to the first conversation. A recruiter skims it in under a minute, often after an automated filter has scanned it. For someone entering AI engineering without a previous AI job title, the resume has to lead with evidence: projects with measured results, a merged open source PR, a link to a portfolio that backs everything up.

This lesson shows you how to write a one-page resume for AI roles that does that.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- What recruiters and hiring managers look for in the first pass.
- A clean, one-page, machine-readable layout.
- Writing bullets that show impact with numbers.
- Presenting projects as experience when you're changing careers.
- Tailoring your resume to a specific posting.
- Common mistakes that get resumes rejected.

### Key ideas

- **Assume a six-second skim, then a closer read.** Put your target role, your portfolio link and your strongest evidence where the eye lands first, at the top.
- **One page, simple layout.** Use a single column, standard section headings, a common font and no tables or graphics that automated applicant tracking systems (ATS) can't parse. Export to PDF.
- **Bullets show impact, not duties.** A good pattern is "accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z." "Built a RAG support agent that answered 84% of 250 held-out questions correctly at $0.40 per 1,000 queries" beats "Worked on a chatbot using RAG."
- **Projects are experience.** Without an AI job title, a "Projects" section near the top carries your resume. List your Capstone and two other showcase projects, each with a one-line description, measured results and a link. Include your merged open source PR.
- **Transferable experience counts.** A previous career in support, operations, teaching or sales is valuable for AI roles, especially forward-deployed ones. Describe it in terms of outcomes and the domain knowledge you bring.
- **Tailor for every application.** Mirror the posting's language for skills you genuinely have, reorder bullets so the most relevant come first, and cut what doesn't serve that role. Your Role Map tells you which skills matter most.
- **Never overclaim.** Anything on your resume is fair game in an interview. If you list a tool, be ready to talk about what you built with it.

### Assignment

<div class="lesson-content__panel" markdown="1">

1. Read the [Tech Interview Handbook resume guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/), a free, thorough guide to software engineering resumes.
   - Read all of it, and pay special attention to the sections on writing bullet points and on what to include for projects.
1. Read the "About" and project pages of three engineers whose work you admire (for example, people you found in Learning in Public), and note how they describe their projects in one line.
1. Rewrite each of your three showcase projects as two or three resume bullets using the "accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z" pattern. Every bullet must include a number.
1. Draft a one-page resume with a projects-first layout. Paste its text into a plain text editor and check that it still reads in the right order; if it doesn't, an ATS will scramble it too.
